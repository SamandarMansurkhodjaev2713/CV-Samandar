02 / 05

Context

What it is

Birthday Agent is a private corporate Telegram system that maintains an employee registry, previews Excel-import changes before applying them, publishes scheduled greetings and preserves a complete trail of administrative actions.

The problem it solves

A small manual mistake has a high public cost: a person can be missed, greeted twice, assigned the wrong date or exposed through unnecessary personal data. A failure between schedule evaluation and delivery also makes an ordinary cron job unsafe.

Architecture

A pure domain layer is isolated from Telegram, PostgreSQL, Excel and the system clock. Application services own transactions and authorization, the scheduler takes a session-level advisory lock, and a database uniqueness constraint rejects a second publication even after retries or concurrent starts.

Why this architecture

The scheduler lock and event uniqueness are independent protections against different failure classes. Import creates a change draft and mutates nothing before confirmation; an append-only audit trail, migrations and verified restore procedures make operation observable rather than dependent on one healthy process.

Evidence

Strengths

This is not a reminder bot but a compact reliable HR workflow. Unknown spreadsheet columns survive an import/export round trip, gender is never inferred from a name, age is never published, and ambiguous rows are delegated to a person instead of being silently guessed.

What it proves

The case demonstrates full-cycle ownership across imperfect business data, domain invariants, Telegram UX, transactions, security, containers, migrations, recovery and QA evidence in one maintainable system.

Quality · QA

How quality was engineered

The 718 tests are risk-layered: 466 unit, 59 Excel, 69 integration and 114 handler checks. Domain and application coverage have a separate enforced threshold; 25 discovered defects were fixed and 24 received a regression test. Telegram networking and clocks are isolated, while critical scenarios run against real PostgreSQL.

Context

What it is

Wedding Invitations Uzbekistan is a private wedding-site platform for Uzbekistan. It is designed as one journey from selecting an independently authored visual direction and editing together to private publishing, a personal guest link and RSVP without a mandatory account.

The problem it solves

A beautiful page is not enough when two people edit content, an invitation exists in several languages, guests receive personal access and responses must enter a controlled list. Without publication versions and explicit privacy boundaries, preview drifts from the live page, stale links expose too much and repeated RSVP creates conflicting data.

Architecture

The monorepo separates public web, admin, API, invitation read plane and worker surfaces. Typed domain contracts define workspaces, locales and the publication format; PostgreSQL/Drizzle stores state and audit while an immutable S3 adapter stores versioned artefacts. Couple magic links use keyed digests, exact-origin CSRF and bounded sessions; the public reader accepts only validated current/N−1 formats.

Why this architecture

The public page reads an immutable artefact rather than a working draft, so the editor can evolve without breaking invitations already sent. Separate couple, guest and staff trust boundaries reduce access surface, while all three locales pass through one contract instead of drifting copies.

Evidence

Strengths

The product combines local cultural fit with serious platform engineering without falling into generic wedding aesthetics. Ten art directions are independently authored systems, while publishing, privacy, RSVP and operations remain shared and verifiable.

What it proves

The case demonstrates product and platform ownership at the same time: art direction, multilingual UX, domain modelling, auth/security, publication protocols, data migrations, operations and QA are governed through one decision system.
